Why people join

A school for the whole of a life, where no one can take the keys.

For three centuries fraternities have taught through degrees, sponsorship and the ballot. OpenMasonry keeps that school, and removes what lets a few capture it: secret government, discretionary rules and permanent power.

“The royal secret is that no one should hold it alone.”

Lesson of the 32nd degree

  • A path worth walking

    Thirty-three degrees, each with a virtue to practice, an art to study and a full public study guide. From grammar and rhetoric in the 1st to guardianship of the Constitution in the 33rd.

  • Equals, not ranks

    Degrees grant duties, never extra votes. A Master Builder of one year and the 33rd degree carry the same single vote.

  • Work that outlives you

    Every degree asks for a public masterwork that improves the commons: an essay, code, a translation, a review. It stays yours, under your name, and the fraternity builds itself through it.

  • A lodge without gatekeepers

    Each degree has its own room for conversation and mentoring. Everything happens online, at your pace, and no meetings are required.

  • Rules you can check

    Every admission, exam and vote is a signed public record. Nobody can quietly change the rules, rig a ballot or sell a rank.

What it asks of you

  • To be an adult who comes in good faith, and to sign the Landmarks.
  • To write, in your own words, a first public reflection.
  • To keep faith: with your work, with those you sponsor, and with the truth.

The 33 degrees

    
What you learn, degree by degree.

    
Six houses, from self-knowledge to the guardianship of the Constitution. Each degree names a virtue to practice, an art to study, a lesson and a masterwork. Choose any degree for its full card.

    

    

      
      
Not affiliated with any grand lodge or Masonic body. OpenMasonry borrows the structure of the degrees, never any ritual. The historical names appear for reference only, and the whole teaching is public.

    

  






  

    
The project

    
An institution kept as a Git repository.

    
OpenMasonry is also an open-source experiment in governance. Its state is never stored: anyone rebuilds it from one signed JSON file per event. The runtime is the Python standard library and OpenSSH, nothing else, under the MIT license.

    
    
      
  1. Someone acts
    A member, helped by their agent, drafts one entry (a petition, a vote, an exam) and signs it with their own SSH key.
    2. 
      
  2. The Tyler judges
    A pull request carries the entry. The judge check replays the whole ledger with the base branch's verifier, reading the pull request as data and never running it.
    3. 
      
  3. The chain grows
    If the protocol allows it, the entry joins the ledger, committing to the hash of the one before. No maintainer can admit anyone by merging.
    4. 
      
  4. Anyone replays
    The Observatory rebuilds the same state from scratch after every change, and so can you, offline.
    5. 
    

    

      
Ballot
Commit, then reveal, in fixed windows. An admission fails when black balls reach one fifth of the votes cast.

      
Lot
Examiners and the 33 council seats are drawn from a public drand beacon about 8 hours in the future, over a pool frozen in advance.

      
Light
A public score of service that fades with a half-life of 180 days. It never weighs a vote.

      
Landmarks
Seven principles pinned in the first entry that no ballot can ever change.

      
Rooms
Closed but never secret: their rules and rolls are public, and nothing said in them has force.

      
Governed files
The Constitution, the degree canon and the protocol core change only by amendment ballot.

One exception, stated openly

      
The Custodian can repair, never grant.

      
When something goes wrong (a stolen key, a ballot captured by fraud, an attack), the ledger is never rewritten. A Custodian makes a correction: a new signed, public entry on one of four closed grounds, which Master Builders can stop by objection.

      

        
A correction can
  • freeze a key for 30 days
  • stay one ballot for 14 days, once
  • halt the ledger for 14 days under attack
  • after public notice, undo a recent admission below the 3rd degree

        
A correction never
  • admits, advances or appoints anyone
  • changes a vote, a key or a Landmark
  • stops a member from leaving or voting
  • takes effect in secret

      

    

    

      
The office ends on its own

      
    
        
  • 180 dayswithout a signed sign of life
    • 
        
  • 7 yearsat the end of its single term
    • 
        
  • Sunsetonce seven active Master Builders who are not founders, and four witnesses, exist
    • 
        
  • MajorityMaster Builders can remove it in a ballot no correction can touch
    • 
      

      
Today the founder is the only Master Builder, so no one can yet object to a correction. The repository says so plainly; every new Master Builder makes the exception smaller.
